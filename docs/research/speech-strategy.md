# Speech composition strategy: how strong policy debaters build each speech

*Research record, 2026-09-25. Written by a research agent from the sources listed at the end (all opened on that date unless noted); reviewed and adopted into Clash's rules (see docs/PROJECT_RECORD.md). Quotes are limited to short lines; everything else is paraphrased with its source.*

I read about 50 sources. Twelve key PDFs were read in full: the NSDA and UIL rules, Bellon's manual, Snider's book, the two NAUDL coaching guides, four Rostrum columns (three by Cheshier, one by Grodd), and a tournament judge guide.

**Evidence tags:**
- **[R]** Rule written down by NSDA, UIL, a state association, or a tournament.
- **[P]** Widely taught: two or more independent coaching sources agree.
- **[C]** One coach's or judge's view.
- **[D]** Measured data.

Citations like [S#] match the numbered source list near the end.

Sources are paraphrased; direct quotation is kept to one short line.

## 0. Two findings to read first

**1. Using the tool during a real round probably breaks tournament rules.**
- **[R] NSDA device rules.** These rules cover Policy at Nationals, and districts by default. Competitors may not receive competitive help from non-competitors: arguments, advice on what to run, CX questions, and "other information not generated by the participating competitors in your round." The penalty is disqualification from the tournament [S50].
- **[R] Ohio (OSDA)** bans generative AI at any time during a debate round [S56].
- **[R] Texas (UIL).** The 2012 CX manual bans coaching while a debate is in progress and requires wireless to be turned off [S52].
- **[R] NSDA's AI rule** (2023, carried into the 2025 Nationals): AI may point students to sources, but AI may not be cited, and any quoted or paraphrased evidence needs a real original source [S57, S58].
- **NSDA leadership, November 2024:** they rejected an outright ban. They also said AI should not write speeches in events where the speech must be the student's own work [S51].
- **News report, May 2026:** students say in-round AI use gets teams disqualified. This is student-sourced, not an NSDA statement [S59].
- **What I could not confirm:** the current NSDA National Tournament AI page.

**What this means for the plan:** add a rule-set gate for each tournament. Also consider positioning the product for practice rounds, speech redos, drills, and coach review.

**2. Almost nothing about how a speech is built is a formal rule.**
- The rules fix only times, prep, CX order, prompting, and device use [S49, S52].
- "No new arguments in rebuttals" shows up in a tournament's judge instructions [S55] and in teaching materials, not in the NSDA or UIL rule text.
- One judge guide says the only fixed rules are speech times and that someone has to win [S55].

So the drafter's rules should be adjustable norms, tuned to each judge's paradigm.

## 1. Format facts [R]

- **Speech times:** 8-minute constructives, 3-minute cross-examinations, 5-minute rebuttals. Rebuttal order is 1NR, 1AR, 2NR, 2AR [S49, S52].
- **Prep time:** NSDA's 2019–20 manual gives 5 minutes per team [S49]. UIL allows up to 8 [S52], and one 2023–24 tournament guide uses 8 [S55]. I could not confirm NSDA's current figure.
- **Prompting:** NSDA discourages a partner prompting the speaker but does not ban it [S49]. UIL treats prompting as a loss [S52]. UIL does not count roadmaps against speech time, but they must not be abused [S52].
- **CX order:** the person who just spoke answers. The questioner is the opponent who is not about to speak, so the next speaker can prep [S2].
- **Judge paradigms:** Policy judges at NSDA Nationals must fill out paradigm questionnaires, which the tool could read in [S51].

## 2. Speech by speech

### 1AC

**(a) What it must contain**
- [P] Harms (significance), inherency, a plan, and solvency, each backed by evidence [S3, S6].
- Two common layouts: stock-issue contentions, or inherency plus plan plus advantages [S2].
- [P] The plan text is the center of the debate. Topicality is judged on the plan, not the advantages, so be able to defend every word [S2, S26].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- [P] Two or three advantages with several internal links each, rather than many thin impacts [S26, S30].
- [P] Mix impact types: fast, probable, and systemic [S30].
- [P] Build in pre-empts to the arguments the negative is likely to run, so the 2AC can point back to 1AC evidence instead of reading new cards [S3, S6, S9, S26, S30].
- [C] Write the plan last, from the solvency evidence. Check each word for exposure to a plan-inclusive counterplan (PIC) [S30].
- [C] Pick the affirmative the way you'd pick a negative strategy: aim at what this opponent handles worst [S29].
- [P] Read the plan slowly. Read author qualifications aloud [S6].
- [C] Between tournaments, cut cards you never use and move cards you always read in the 2AC into the 1AC [S6, S9].

**(c) Time**
- Fully scripted and timed to 8:00 [S3, S6].

**(d) Automatic checks**
- Stock issues and plan text are all present.
- Every advantage has an internal link and an impact.
- Every plan word is supported by solvency evidence (topicality risk).
- At least one pre-empt exists for each of the top predicted negative positions (from the opponent's caselist).
- Length fits 8:00 at the configured words per minute.
- Advantage labels are 1–3 words.

**(e) Sources:** S2, S3, S6, S9, S26, S29, S30

### 1NC

**(a) What it must contain**
- [P] Every position the negative might use later, each as a complete "shell" [S1, S2, S3, S5]:

| Position | Required parts |
|---|---|
| Disadvantage (DA) | uniqueness, link, internal link when needed, impact |
| Counterplan (CP) | text, competition (can't coexist with the plan, or a net benefit), solvency |
| Topicality (T) | interpretation/definition, violation, standards, voting issue |
| Kritik (K) | link, impact, alternative, plus framework as needed |
| Theory | same parts as T |

- [P] Roadmap: say how many off-case positions, read them first, then go to the case [S1, S3, S5].
- [P] Judges expect the 1NC to clash with the case too [S3, S5].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- [P] Give each generic position one sentence explaining the link to this specific affirmative [S5].
- [P] Avoid repeating the same links or impacts across positions, and avoid contradictions [S5].
- [P] Build positions you can drop ("kick") cleanly later [S5].
- [P] Know the status of each CP or K (conditional or not); the 2AC will ask [S1, S2].
- [C] Every off-case position should be a real option to go for in the 2NR. Favor offense over piles of defense [S35].
- [C] A good analytic beats a weak card. Pick positions that cost the 1AR as much time as they cost you [S33].
- [C] Adapt to the actual 1AC instead of reading a canned speech [S10].

**(c) Time**
- [C] Novice guidance: roughly half case, half off-case [S5].
- [C] Use 10% of prep or less, and time your shells in advance [S3].
- Coaches' examples of circuit 1NCs include 8 off-case plus 15 case arguments [S20] and 10 off-case [S9, S14]. I found no dataset on this.

**(d) Automatic checks**
- Shell completeness by type.
- A "could this win in the 2NR?" score for each position.
- Duplicate-link detector.
- Contradiction detector (for example, one DA says a bill passes, another says it fails), unless the positions are conditional.
- The affirmative-specific link sentence is present.
- The roadmap count matches the positions actually read.

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S5, S9, S10, S14, S20, S33, S35

### 2AC

**(a) What it must do**
- [P] Answer every 1NC position and defend the case. It is the affirmative's last constructive speech [S2, S3, S6].
- [P] It also has to hold up against the next 13 minutes of negative speeches [S2].
- [C] Debate 101 estimates that dropping a complete 1NC argument loses about 90% of rounds [S1].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- **Format**
  - [P] Number the answers on each position. Give each a 1–2 word label, then a tag, then the card. Analytic answers get a tag, a short explanation, and an impact [S1, S3, S6].
  - [P] Say where you are, then answer. Don't re-explain the negative's argument [S3, S12].
  - [C] An argument is at least 10 words [S16].
  - [C] Organize answers in groups (uniqueness, link, impact), best answer first [S14, S9].
- **Offense and turns**
  - [P] Mix answer types and include offense: link turns, impact turns, add-ons, and disadvantages to counterplans [S3, S6].
  - [P] Never link-turn and impact-turn the same DA; that is a "double turn" [S2, S3, S45].
  - [C] When extending a link turn, also extend the uniqueness argument [S7].
  - [C] Turn only the DAs (answer them with offense alone) when the 1NC has no counterplans [S14].
- **Permutations and theory**
  - [C] A permutation must explain how it avoids the counterplan's net benefit. A bare "perm do both" is not an argument [S17].
  - [P] Ask the CP's status in cross-ex and keep conditionality theory ready as a backup [S1, S2, S14].
- **Advantage counterplans**
  - [C] Lead with solvency deficits: which internal link the CP misses, why it matters, and how much risk remains. Add add-ons and DAs to the CP. Don't lean on theory [S16, S25].
- **Coverage choices**
  - [C] Concede weak advantages rather than spreading thin [S16].
  - [C] Against many off-case positions, give 6–8 answers per DA (mostly link defense), and make deliberate coverage choices [S14].
  - [C] Against very fast teams, give your best four answers, not all fifteen [S9].

**(c) Time**
- [C] Snider: spend at least as much time on the case as the negative did. Aim to be about 25% through your flows at 2:00 and 50% at 4:00. Use 25% of prep or less [S3].
- [C] NAUDL: roughly match the 1NC's case/off-case split, leaning toward off-case because 1AC evidence covers the case [S6].
- These two views conflict, so make this setting adjustable.

**(d) Automatic checks**
- **Critical:** any 1NC position with zero answers.
- Double-turn detector.
- Bare-perm detector.
- Analytic answers have a warrant (a "because" clause, at least 10 words).
- Link turns are paired with uniqueness.
- Contradictions across flows (for example, conceding a solvency argument that kills your own advantage) [S3].
- Case-time ratio against the setting above.
- Answers ordered best-first.
- Every "voting issue" claim gets an answer [S3].
- Mark the answers most likely to matter in the 2AR for the 1AR [S7].

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S6, S7, S9, S12, S14, S16, S17, S25, S45

### Negative block (2NC + 1NR)

**(a) What it must do**
- [P] Treat the 2NC and 1NR as one 13-minute speech. Split the positions and never cover the same ground twice [S1, S2, S3, S15, S46].
- [P] On every position you extend, answer every 2AC argument, especially turns and theory [S1, S3, S5].
- [P] The 1NR is the last line of defense against affirmative offense on positions being dropped [S1].
- The 2NC may add new arguments [S2]. Some judges think new off-case positions in the 2NC are abusive [S54]; Grodd defends them [S10].
- The 1NR counts as a rebuttal, so new arguments there should only answer the 2AC [S2, S27].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- **Structure**
  - [P] Start each extended position with a short overview, one sentence each on link, uniqueness, and impact, plus case turns. Then go line by line [S5, S8, S15].
  - [P] Extend 1NC cards by author and warrant, and add more evidence where needed [S3, S5].
- **Evidence**
  - [C] Read about 2.5 cards for every 2AC card on contested points [S15].
  - [C] Stop once the 2AC has conceded a part [S15].
  - [C] Don't read a lone impact card; it invites the 1AR to answer with a stack of impact turns [S15].
- **Dropping ("kicking") a position**
  - [P] Concede only defensive answers (no link, not unique). Never leave a turn standing [S3, S5, S15, S31].
  - [P] Drop a counterplan by conceding the perm or competition [S3, S31].
  - [P] Announce the kick slowly and clearly [S15].
  - [P] Dropping a topicality argument the 1AR spent a minute on frees up 2NR time [S5].
- **The 1NR**
  - [P] Keep it substantive and hard to predict, so the 1AR can't shortchange it [S1, S15, S21].
  - [C] Give the 1NR a position the 2AC read a lot of evidence on. The 1NR has about 14 minutes of effective prep: 2AC cross-ex, the 2NC, and 2NC cross-ex [S21].
  - [C] The 1NR takes no prep time [S3].
- **Strategy**
  - [C] Settle on a main option for the 2NR early, but don't reveal it [S5].
  - [C] Choose early whether to go deep on a few positions or broad on many [S15].
  - [P] Start weighing impacts in the block [S3, S5].

**(c) Time**
- [C] The 2NC extends 2–3 off-case positions [S5].
- [C] The 2NC uses 40% of prep or less [S3], or saves at least half [S15].
- [C] The 1NR uses none [S3].

**(d) Automatic checks**
- The 2NC and 1NR don't duplicate each other.
- **Critical:** any 2AC argument left unanswered on an extended flow, especially turns, theory, and add-ons.
- Offense left standing on dropped positions.
- The kick method is valid (only defensive answers conceded).
- 1NR arguments are not new.
- Overview length.
- Cards extended without explaining their warrant.

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S5, S8, S10, S15, S21, S27, S31, S46, S54

### 1AR

**(a) What it must do**
- [P] Answer everything extended in the block, including new 2NC arguments.
- [P] Extend the 2AC arguments and case pieces the 2AR will need. Judges screen out new 2AR claims, so anything dropped here is very hard to get back [S1, S2, S3, S6, S7].
- [P] Always answer arguments judges decide first: topicality, case turns, and some kritik or decision-rule claims [S7].
- Correction to a common summary: NAUDL says the 1AR does not need to extend every 2AC argument, only the most important [S6].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- **Triage**
  - [P] Choose a set of 2AC answers on each position (for example, answers 2, 4, and 6). Make the set varied (link, uniqueness, impact; theory plus substance) so the 2AR has options [S3, S6, S11].
  - [C] While the 2AC speaks, circle the 2–3 best answers on each position and prep those first [S7].
- **Extend, don't repeat**
  - [P] Respond to what the block said and show why your argument still beats it [S6, S7, S13].
  - [C] Explaining a complex point once can save time later [S7].
- **Grouping**
  - [P] Group arguments that share one answer, such as topicality standards or case presses. Don't group major positions, or the speech becomes a list of tags [S7, S9].
  - [C] One coach disagrees: grouping weakens answers, and the work should be moved into the 2AC [S40].
- **Theory and perms**
  - [C] Extend theory only if you need it. If you do, spend about a minute (200–250 words): your offense, why it's a voting issue, answers to their arguments, and impact comparison [S12, S13].
  - [C] Keep a perm alive against counterplans. Conditionality theory eats time [S7].
- **Word economy**
  - [P] Use abbreviations, drop filler phrases, put key words first in labels, and never repeat yourself [S3, S6, S11].
  - [C] Point back to 1AC and 2AC evidence. Read a new card only when something critical depends on it [S3, S7].
- **Order**
  - [P] Topicality and other threshold issues first, then DAs and CPs, then case. Never leave all-or-nothing issues for the end [S3, S6].
  - [C] Plan the speech order with your partner [S7].
- **Opportunities and risks**
  - [C] Use the negative's contradictions (inherency vs. DA, solvency vs. DA, DA vs. DA) carefully [S3].
  - [C] Commonly dropped here: "DA turns the case" arguments, kritik overviews, and voting issues [S13].

**(c) Time**
- [P] Default: split time in proportion to the block's split, then adjust for how important and how good each argument is [S6].
- [C] Cheshier warns that copying the block's split exactly is a sign the 1AR didn't prep [S7].
- [C] Phillips' budget: about 1:30 on politics comes out to three arguments of about 125 words each [S11].
- [C] Use 50% of aff prep or less [S3]. Minimize prep, but don't give the speech without having flowed the block [S7].
- [D] In a Team Policy survey, 60% of more than 150 debaters rated the 1AR hardest. They averaged about two cards per 1AR, roughly 20% of the speech [S40].

**(d) Automatic checks**
- Every block argument is answered or explicitly grouped.
- Every argument marked as a likely 2AR choice is extended with its warrant.
- The 1AR isn't just repeating the 2AC: flag high text similarity to the 2AC with no reference to the block's answers.
- Link turns are extended with uniqueness.
- Threshold issues are not placed last.
- Major positions are not grouped away.
- Extended theory includes a voting issue and impact comparison.
- Arguments that aren't in the 2AC are flagged as new; they are allowed only against new block arguments.
- Word budget for each argument.

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S6, S7, S9, S11, S12, S13, S40

### 2NR

**(a) What it must do**
- [P] Narrow to what wins ("collapse").
- [P] Extend a complete argument: every part of the DA, CP, K, or topicality argument.
- [P] Answer everything the 1AR said on it.
- [P] Tell the judge exactly why the negative wins; Snider calls this writing the ballot for the judge [S1, S3, S5, S36].
- [P] No new arguments beyond the block, except answers to new 1AR arguments and impact comparison [S2, S3].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- **What to go for**
  - [P] One main offensive position, occasionally two, plus case arguments where the affirmative is weakest [S1, S5, S36].
  - [C] If going for topicality, most judges want the entire 2NR on it [S3].
  - [P] Two routes: "win the drop" (lead with the dropped argument, explain why it alone is enough, and weigh it against the case) or "win the position" [S3].
  - [C] Find the 1AR's biggest mistake [S34].
  - [P] Adapt to where the 1AR's coverage was thin [S5].
- **Weighing**
  - [P] Compare impacts on magnitude, probability, and timeframe, using the dimensions you actually win [S44].
  - [P] Include "turns the case" arguments and direct comparison to the affirmative's impacts [S15, S32].
  - [P] Layer "even if" statements: even if you lose one piece, you still win [S3, S42].
  - [C] Batterman: sorting arguments into offense and defense is a starting point, not a decision rule. Compare explicitly [S22].
  - [C] Phillips thinks "turns the case" is overrated unless the other side dropped it [S18]. This conflicts with the widely taught view above.
- **Structure and the 2AR**
  - [P] Short overview (NAUDL: 30 seconds or less), then line by line, with no repetition [S5, S8].
  - [P] Predict and close off the likely 2AR routes. Ask the judge to ignore anything that can't be traced to the 1AR [S1, S3, S5].
- **Judge kick** (the judge may drop the counterplan and compare the plan to the status quo)
  - [C] Ask for it explicitly and justify it; some judges won't do it on their own [S24].
  - One paradigm snippet says that judge applies it automatically unless told otherwise [S63]. I found no verified source on whether it has to be requested in the block.
- **Kritik 2NR**
  - [C] An overview of the kritik's thesis, the framing question, specific links (including links to things the 1AR said), impacts compared to the case, how the alternative interacts with the plan, and why the perm doesn't avoid the link [S32].
- **Prep**
  - [C] Use all remaining prep. Get the big picture set first, then the details [S3].

**(c) Time**
- Overview 30 seconds or less [S5]. All 5 minutes on topicality if going for topicality [S3].

**(d) Automatic checks**
- Flag more than two main offensive positions.
- **Critical:** every 1AR argument on the chosen flows is answered.
- The extended position has all its parts.
- Explicit impact comparison is present.
- At least one "even if" statement.
- A sentence telling the judge how to vote.
- A list of likely 2AR routes and answers to them.
- Arguments with no source in the block are flagged as new.
- If going for a counterplan while wanting the status quo as a fallback, a judge-kick request is present.

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S5, S8, S15, S18, S22, S24, S32, S34, S36, S42, S44, S63

### 2AR

**(a) What it must do**
- [P] Beat what the 2NR went for, re-establish the case's advantage, and explain why the affirmative wins.
- [P] Every argument must trace back to the 1AR [S1, S2, S3, S6].
- [P] Judges allow some development of 1AR answers, but not brand-new responses [S1].

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- [P] Set your own order: start with the strongest reason to vote affirmative, not the 2NR's order [S3, S6].
- [C] Typically start by rebuilding the case and going for one advantage [S37].
- [P] Compare the two worlds (plan vs. status quo or counterplan). Concede where you must with "even if" framing, such as "even with half solvency, the plan beats the status quo" [S3, S42].
- [P] Point back explicitly to 1AR answers [S6].
- [P] Use only the strongest 1AR answers. Be selective and a bit slower [S3, S6].
- [P] Make sure no negative offense goes unanswered, and press any affirmative offense the negative dropped [S1].
- [C] Script the overview during the 2NR's prep [S8].
- [C] In a messy debate, spend some efficiency to make it clear for the judge [S20].

**(c) Time**
- [C] Match the 2NR's emphasis [S3]. Use all remaining prep [S3].

**(d) Automatic checks**
- **Critical:** every argument traces to the 1AR.
- Everything the 2NR extended is covered.
- A sentence telling the judge how to vote.
- A two-worlds comparison.
- "Even if" statements.
- Time emphasis matches the 2NR.
- Not going for too many things.

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S6, S8, S20, S37, S42

### Cross-examination (all four periods)

**(a) Obligations**
- [P] The partner of the questioner preps during CX [S1, S2, S3].
- [R, local] A tournament judge guide says concessions count only if they are repeated in a speech [S55]. Make this a hard requirement for the drafter.

**(b) How strong debaters approach it**
- **Purposes**, in priority order: clarify, expose errors, set up arguments, get admissions, save prep [S2]. Question types are clarification, perception, and strategy [S38].
- [P] Prepare questions in advance. Ask closed, yes-or-no questions. Know the answer before you ask. Ask about parts of the card that weren't read aloud. Listen, and use the answers [S1, S2, S38].
- **By period:**
  - 2NC questions the 1AC: plan wording, missing signposts, the core claim of each advantage, and links for planned DAs [S2, S3].
  - 1AC questions the 1NC: topicality violation and standards, DA links, thresholds, and impacts, why the CP is better, and the status of each CP or K [S1, S2].
  - 1NC questions the 2AC: answers the negative missed, a copy of their blocks, and any concessions that open a clean way to drop a position [S2, S3].
  - 2AC questions the 2NC: clarify things for the 1AR [S1, S3].
- [P] Don't ask the 2AC whether they dropped something; it gives them a chance to fix it [S1].
- [C] Lead the opponent step by step toward a damaging admission. Use all of your CX time [S2].
- [C] Against fast teams, ask narrow questions about specific unclear answers [S9].
- **Answering:** be honest, don't claim more than your evidence says, decline hypotheticals, and point back to your evidence [S2].

**(d) Automatic checks**
- Every planned question is tied to a target argument.
- Open-ended questions are limited to clarification.
- Concessions are captured and must be restated in the next speech.
- The status question is asked whenever a CP or K was read.
- In your own answers, no commitments beyond your evidence.

**(e) Sources:** S1, S2, S3, S9, S38, S55

## 3. Cross-cutting skills

**Flowing and signposting [P]**
- Use a separate flow sheet for each off-case position and each advantage, a column per speech, and room between arguments. Record tag, author, and year [S1, S3].
- Roadmap first. Then signpost by flow name and answer number, and avoid saying "next" instead of a number [S3].
- [C] Answer 1AC pre-empts on the case flow. Keep impact comparison in one place. Use consistent labels across speeches [S19].
- [C] Coaches report that signposting has worn away at speed, which confuses judges [S20].

**Extensions [P]**
- Snider's model has three parts: assertion (the label), reasoning (why it's true), and evidence [S3].
- A real extension answers the opponent's latest response and explains why yours is better; it is not a summary [S3, S6].
- Extend cards by author and warrant [S2, S5].
- Numbered lists are independent reasons, so each must be answered. Lettered chains need only one link broken [S3].

**Analytics (arguments made without cards)**
- [P] They are legitimate and often strong when they have a warrant [S5, S33].
- Format: tag, short explanation, and impact [S1].
- [C] At least 10 words [S16].
- **Answering them** [P]: contradict, deny relevance, undercut assumptions, cross-apply, outweigh, compare evidence quality, attack the author, or concede and turn it to your advantage [S2].
- Dismiss incomplete analytics quickly [S14], saying briefly what's missing (no warrant) [S39].
- **Flowing them:** record them even without a citation, and flag unclear ones for CX questions [S9].
- [C] Some judges read analytics from the speech document. Don't rely on that; deliver them audibly [S48].

**"New" arguments in rebuttals [P]**
- See the 1NR, 2NR, and 2AR sections above. The 1AR may answer new block arguments [S2, S43].
- Impact comparison gets limited room to be new [S8]. Some judges look closely at overviews for hidden new claims [S8].
- [C] Cheshier argues that conceding the opponent's arguments is never new, though some judges treat big concessions as new [S9].

**Dropped arguments**
- [P] Judges treat an unanswered argument as won, but still decide how much it matters [S2].
- [P] The 2AR can't answer what the 1AR dropped [S2].
- **Limits** [C]: a concession is worth only as much as its warrant [S39]. Some judges want the claim, warrant, impact, and implication all extended (from Tabroom search snippets) [S63].
- Implicit concessions count too; for example, a link turn implicitly concedes the impact [S28].

**Judge adaptation [P]**
- **Lay judges:** conversational speed, big-picture story, no jargon, and probability over magnitude [S47].
- **Clarity over speed** in UIL judge statements [S54]. UIL's judging criteria lower speaker points when speed gets in the way [S53].
- [C] Against fast teams, be clearly slower but more efficient; the contrast helps [S9].
- Research judges before the round [S3]. Read the NSDA paradigm questionnaires into the tool [S51].

**Prep and partner roles [C]**
- **Snider's prep caps:**
  - 1NC: 10% or less.
  - 2AC: 25% or less.
  - 2NC: 40% or less.
  - 1AR: 50% or less.
  - 1NR: none.
  - 2NR and 2AR: all remaining prep.
- He also assigns tasks to each partner during every speech [S3].
- Don't let your partner script your 1AR [S7]. Partners must tell each other about dropped arguments [S20].

**Speed and word economy**
- [D] The speed figures are in section 4.
- [P] Say it once. Cut prepositional phrases and "to be" verbs [S1, S9].

## 5. What the flow data needs so these checks can run

Store each argument with:
- An ID, the speech it came from, and which flow sheet it's on.
- Its type: card, analytic, theory, perm, link turn, impact turn, defense, or overview.
- Its label, author, and year.
- Its warrant text.
- A parent ID (the argument it answers) and a group ID (for grouped answers).
- Its status in each later speech: answered, extended, dropped, kicked, or conceded.
- Its word count.
- Strategic flags: "could win in the 2NR" and "could win in the 2AR."

The two traceability checks work off the parent links:
- **"New in the 2AR":** a 2AR argument with no ancestor in the 1AR.
- **"The 1AR dropped something the 2AR needs":** a flagged 2AC argument with no 1AR extension.

**Related research:**
- DeepDebater already runs complete policy rounds and is a useful benchmark [S60].
- The "Argument Collapse" paper found that LLM-written essays converge on the same few main arguments: 3.4% of LLM main arguments were unique, versus 65.3% for humans [S62]. Add a check for argument variety to 1NC and 2AC drafts.

## 6. The 20 most valuable rules for an AI drafter

1. **Traceability.** 2AR arguments must trace to the 1AR, and 2NR arguments to the block (or to new 1AR arguments). New 1NR arguments may only answer the 2AC. Impact comparison is the limited exception. [P]
2. **2AC coverage.** Every 1NC position gets numbered, labeled answers, best first, including at least one offensive answer. A position with zero answers is a critical error. [P]
3. **No double turns.** Never link-turn and impact-turn the same DA. [P]
4. **Every analytic and perm needs a warrant.** Claim, reason, and implication; no bare "perm do both"; 10 words minimum. [P/C]
5. **1AR breadth and depth.** Answer every block argument, and extend, with warrants, the varied 2AC answers the 2AR might need. [P]
6. **Extend, don't repeat.** Engage the latest answer. Extend by author and warrant, not just the answer number. [P]
7. **The block is one speech.** Split positions between the 2NC and 1NR and never duplicate. [P]
8. **Kick cleanly.** Before dropping a position, neutralize any offense on it: concede defense, never turns, and answer theory and add-ons. [P]
9. **Complete 1NC shells.** Every 1NC position is complete, affirmative-specific, and a realistic 2NR option. [P/C]
10. **2NR collapse.** Go for one main position (two at most) and answer everything the 1AR said on it. [P]
11. **Weigh explicitly in the last two rebuttals.** Magnitude, probability, timeframe, and turns-case, compared to the other side, with "even if" layers. [P]
12. **Tell the judge how to vote.** Last rebuttals include an explicit ballot sentence and a two-worlds comparison. [P]
13. **The 2NR closes off 2AR routes.** Name the likely routes, answer them, and ask the judge to reject anything not traceable to the 1AR. [P]
14. **2AR agenda.** Lead with the strongest reason to vote affirmative, but cover everything the 2NR went for, matching its emphasis. [P/C]
15. **Dropped arguments.** A dropped argument wins only as much as its warrant. To cash it in: extend it, state what it means for the round, and weigh it. [P/C]
16. **CX sets things up.** Every question ties to a planned argument, and concessions must be restated in the next speech. [P, R-local]
17. **Fit the speech to the time.** Budget words at the configured wpm and put the most important argument first. [D/P]
18. **Adapt to the judge's paradigm.** Lay judges: fewer arguments, big picture, plain language, slower. Flow judges: line by line. [P]
19. **Signpost everything.** Roadmap, flow name, and answer number, with short labels. [P]
20. **No self-contradiction.** Check across flows (inherency, solvency, and DA uniqueness), and exploit the opponent's contradictions. [P]
